Natural Resources for CBSE Class 9: Air, Water and Soil Chapter Guide

Published on October 26, 2026 • Chapter 14 (NCERT) Comprehensive Guide

Natural resources form the backbone of life on Earth and are a critical chapter in the CBSE Class 9 Science curriculum. Chapter 14, titled "Natural Resources," in the NCERT textbook covers three fundamental aspects: Air, Water, and Soil. This chapter is essential for understanding how our planet's resources sustain life and why conservation is necessary. Whether you're preparing for unit tests, periodic exams, or final board assessments, mastering this chapter will significantly boost your science scores.

Understanding Natural Resources: The Foundation (NCERT Chapter 14)

Natural resources are materials or substances that occur naturally and are used by humans for survival and economic benefit. According to the NCERT Class 9 Science textbook, natural resources are classified into three main categories based on their availability and renewal rates: renewable resources, non-renewable resources, and biotic and abiotic resources.

The focus of this chapter is on three essential natural resources—air, water, and soil—that are interconnected through the biosphere. Understanding how these three resources interact and depend on each other is crucial for the exam. Students often face questions in multiple-choice and short-answer formats asking about the composition of these resources, their pollution, conservation methods, and their role in supporting life.

Key Concepts to Focus On:

  • Air: Composition (78% N₂, 21% O₂, 1% other gases), atmospheric layers, and functions
  • Water: Distribution on Earth, freshwater sources, saltwater importance, and the water cycle
  • Soil: Formation, composition, types, and significance in agriculture
  • Interconnection: How air, water, and soil support ecosystems

Air as a Natural Resource: Composition and Pollution

Air is an invisible but vital natural resource that supports all aerobic life on Earth. The NCERT textbook emphasizes that Earth's atmosphere is composed primarily of nitrogen and oxygen, with trace amounts of carbon dioxide, argon, and water vapor. This gaseous envelope not only provides oxygen for respiration but also protects us from harmful ultraviolet radiation through the ozone layer.

Pollution of air is a significant topic in the exam syllabus. Common questions ask about sources of air pollution (industrial emissions, vehicular exhaust, burning of fossil fuels), the greenhouse effect, acid rain, and the depletion of the ozone layer. Students should understand how human activities contribute to air pollution and the consequences of climate change. The chapter emphasizes the importance of reducing carbon footprints and promoting clean energy sources.

Common Exam Questions on Air:

  • What is the composition of air? (2-3 mark question)
  • Define acid rain and its causes (3-5 mark question)
  • How does the greenhouse effect contribute to global warming? (5 mark question)
  • What steps can we take to reduce air pollution? (3 mark question)

Common Mistake to Avoid

Many students confuse the ozone layer (present in the stratosphere at 15-35 km altitude) with ground-level ozone pollution. Remember: the ozone layer protects us from UV radiation, while ground-level ozone is a harmful pollutant. Make sure to clarify this distinction in your answers!

Water: Earth's Most Precious Resource

Water covers approximately 71% of Earth's surface, yet only about 2.5-3% is freshwater. This critical natural resource is essential for all forms of life and is extensively covered in the NCERT chapter. The textbook discusses the distribution of water on Earth, sources of freshwater (glaciers, groundwater, rivers, lakes), and the water cycle (evaporation, condensation, precipitation, and collection).

Water pollution is another major topic emphasized in exams. Students should be familiar with point sources (industries, sewage discharge) and non-point sources (agricultural runoff) of water pollution. The chapter stresses the importance of water conservation methods such as rainwater harvesting, watershed management, and efficient irrigation techniques. Knowledge of dams and their role in water management is also frequently tested.

Key Topics for Exam Preparation:

  • Distribution of water on Earth and freshwater sources (2 mark question)
  • The water cycle and its stages (3 mark question with diagram)
  • Types and sources of water pollution (5 mark question)
  • Methods of water conservation and rainwater harvesting (3-5 mark question)
  • Role of dams in water resource management (2-3 mark question)

A practical tip for exam success: Be prepared to draw and label the water cycle diagram. This is a common question worth 3-5 marks. Also, learn the percentages: 71% water on Earth, 97% saline, 3% freshwater, and of that 3%, 68.7% is locked in ice caps and glaciers.

Soil: The Living Layer Supporting Agriculture

Soil is a dynamic natural resource that forms over thousands of years through the weathering of rocks and the addition of organic matter. The NCERT chapter explains soil composition (minerals, organic matter, water, and air), soil formation processes, and various soil types (sandy, clayey, and loamy). Understanding soil is critical because it directly impacts agriculture, which feeds our population.

The chapter covers soil erosion and degradation, which are major concerns in agriculture. Factors like deforestation, overgrazing, and improper land use practices contribute to soil loss. Students should know conservation methods such as contour plowing, terracing, crop rotation, and afforestation. The importance of soil microorganisms and their role in nutrient cycling is also relevant for board exams.

Topics Frequently Asked in Exams:

  • Composition of soil and soil formation (2-3 mark question)
  • Types of soil and their characteristics (3 mark question with table)
  • Soil erosion: causes and prevention methods (5 mark question)
  • Role of earthworms and microorganisms in soil (2-3 mark question)
  • Importance of soil conservation in agriculture (3 mark question)

Exam Pro Tip

When answering soil-related questions, remember the term "A-horizon" (topsoil with nutrients and organic matter) and "B-horizon" (subsoil). Questions about soil layers often test whether students understand which horizon is most fertile. The A-horizon is the answer most exams are looking for regarding fertility!

Interconnection of Air, Water, and Soil in Ecosystems

The true significance of this chapter lies in understanding how air, water, and soil are interconnected through biogeochemical cycles and ecosystems. The biosphere depends on the continuous cycling of materials and energy through these three resources. The nitrogen cycle, carbon cycle, and water cycle all involve interactions between air, water, and soil. Exams often include 5-mark questions asking students to explain these interconnections and their importance for sustaining life.

From a CBSE exam perspective, expect questions that require you to think holistically about how pollution in one resource affects the others. For example, acid rain (air pollution) affects soil pH and water quality. Industrial effluents in water pollute soil, which in turn affects air quality through methane emissions from soil microorganisms.
