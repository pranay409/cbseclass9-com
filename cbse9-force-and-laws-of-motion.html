Force and Laws of Motion: Newton's Laws with Examples

Published on October 22, 2026

Force and Laws of Motion is a fundamental chapter in CBSE Class 9 Science that forms the foundation for understanding physics. This chapter introduces students to Newton's three laws of motion, which are crucial not only for board exams but also for building a strong conceptual base for higher studies. Whether you're preparing for your periodic tests, pre-board exams, or want to strengthen your understanding, this comprehensive guide covers everything you need to know.

Understanding Force: Definition and Types

NCERT Chapter 9 - Reference

Force is defined as a push or pull that changes the state of rest or motion of an object. In simple terms, force is an interaction that causes acceleration. The SI unit of force is Newton (N), where 1 Newton = 1 kg·m/s².

Types of Forces:

  • Contact Forces: Forces that require physical contact, such as friction, normal force, and applied force
  • Non-Contact Forces: Forces that act without physical contact, such as gravitational force, magnetic force, and electrostatic force

In the context of CBSE Class 9, you'll primarily focus on gravitational force and friction. Understanding these forces is essential as they frequently appear in board exam questions and numericals.

⚡ Key Exam Tip

Common Mistake: Many students confuse force with weight. Weight is the gravitational force acting on an object (W = mg), while force is any push or pull. Remember, force is a vector quantity with both magnitude and direction!

Newton's First Law of Motion: Law of Inertia

NCERT Chapter 9 - Reference

Newton's First Law states: "An object in a state of rest will remain at rest, and an object in motion will continue in motion with constant velocity unless acted upon by an external force."

This law introduces the concept of inertia, which is the tendency of an object to resist changes in its state of motion. Inertia depends on the mass of an object—greater the mass, greater the inertia.

Real-World Examples:

  • When a bus suddenly brakes, passengers tend to move forward due to inertia
  • A book resting on a table remains at rest until someone picks it up
  • When you pull a tablecloth quickly from under dishes, the dishes remain in place due to their inertia
  • A car moving at constant velocity will continue unless the driver applies brakes

This law is crucial for understanding equilibrium and is frequently tested in CBSE board exams through conceptual questions and diagram-based problems.

Newton's Second Law of Motion: F = ma

NCERT Chapter 9 - Reference

Newton's Second Law states: "The acceleration of an object is directly proportional to the net force applied and inversely proportional to its mass."

Mathematically, this is represented as:

F = ma

Where:

  • F = Net force (in Newtons)
  • m = Mass of the object (in kg)
  • a = Acceleration (in m/s²)

This law is the most important and most frequently tested law in CBSE Class 9 exams. Students must be comfortable with numerical problems involving force, mass, and acceleration calculations.

Key Points for Exam Success:

  • Force is a vector quantity; direction matters
  • When multiple forces act on an object, use net force in the equation
  • This law explains why lighter objects accelerate faster than heavier objects when the same force is applied
  • For a given force, doubling the mass halves the acceleration
📝 Common Numerical Pattern

A typical exam question: "A force of 20 N is applied to a 5 kg object. Calculate the acceleration." Using F = ma: a = F/m = 20/5 = 4 m/s². Practice such problems regularly to master this concept!

Newton's Third Law of Motion: Action and Reaction

NCERT Chapter 9 - Reference

Newton's Third Law states: "For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction. They act on different bodies."

This is a crucial point that many students misunderstand. The action and reaction forces:

  • Are equal in magnitude
  • Are opposite in direction
  • Act on different objects
  • Do not cancel each other out

Practical Examples for CBSE Exams:

  • Walking: You push the ground backward (action), and the ground pushes you forward (reaction)
  • Swimming: A swimmer pushes water backward (action), and water pushes the swimmer forward (reaction)
  • Rocket Launch: A rocket expels gases downward (action), and the expelled gases push the rocket upward (reaction)
  • Jumping: You push the Earth downward (action), and Earth pushes you upward (reaction)
  • Collision: Car A exerts force on Car B, and Car B exerts an equal and opposite force on Car A

Board exams often include questions asking students to identify action-reaction pairs and explain why they don't cancel. Understanding this law deeply is essential for scoring well.

💡 Important Concept

Why don't action-reaction pairs cancel? Because they act on different bodies! If you push a wall with your hand, your hand experiences a reaction force from the wall. These forces don't cancel because one acts on your hand and one acts on the wall.

Applications of Newton's Laws in Daily Life

Understanding how Newton's laws apply to everyday situations helps in answering conceptual questions in board exams:

  • Seatbelts in Cars: Seatbelts keep you at rest (relative to car) when brakes are applied, preventing injury (First Law)
  • Heavier Objects Harder to Move: A loaded truck requires more force to accelerate than an empty truck (Second Law)
  • Recoil of Guns: When a gun fires a bullet forward, the gun recoils backward (Third Law)
  • Friction: Friction force opposes motion and must be overcome to move objects (First Law)
