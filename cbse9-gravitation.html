Gravitation for CBSE Class 9: Universal Law and Numerical Problems

Published on October 23, 2026

Understanding Gravitation: The Universal Law

Gravitation is one of the four fundamental forces of nature and forms a crucial part of CBSE Class 9 Science (Chapter 10). The Universal Law of Gravitation, formulated by Sir Isaac Newton, explains how every object in the universe attracts every other object with a force that is directly proportional to their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them.

This concept is essential for board examinations as it appears in both theoretical questions (2-3 marks) and numerical problems (3-5 marks). Understanding gravitation helps explain planetary motion, satellite behavior, and everyday phenomena like why objects fall to the ground.

Newton's Universal Law of Gravitation states: The force of attraction between two bodies is directly proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between their centers.

F = G × (M × m) / r²
Where:
F = Gravitational force (N)
G = Universal gravitational constant (6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²)
M, m = Masses of the two bodies (kg)
r = Distance between centers (m)

Key Concepts from NCERT Chapter 10: Gravitation

The NCERT curriculum emphasizes several critical concepts that students must master for both formative and summative assessments:

  • Mass vs. Weight: Mass is the amount of matter in an object (constant everywhere), while weight is the force exerted by gravity on that mass. Weight = m × g, where g is acceleration due to gravity (9.8 m/s² or 10 m/s²).
  • Acceleration due to Gravity: The value of g varies with altitude and latitude. At Earth's surface, g ≈ 9.8 m/s². This concept is frequently tested in numerical problems.
  • Free Fall: Motion of an object under the influence of gravity alone. All objects fall with the same acceleration (g) regardless of their mass, assuming no air resistance.
  • Thrust and Pressure: Understanding how gravitational force relates to thrust (force per unit area) is essential for solving real-world problems.
  • Buoyancy and Archimedes' Principle: The upward force exerted by a fluid on an immersed object equals the weight of the fluid displaced.
⚠️ Common Mistake: Students often confuse mass and weight. Remember: mass is measured in kilograms (kg) and is the same everywhere, while weight is measured in Newtons (N) and changes with the value of g. On the Moon, your mass remains the same, but your weight is only 1/6th of Earth's weight!

Solving Numerical Problems: Step-by-Step Approach

CBSE board exams typically include 3-5 mark numerical questions on gravitation. Here's a systematic approach to solving them:

Step 1: Identify What You Know
List all given values (masses, distances, gravitational constant). Ensure all units are in SI format.

Step 2: Identify What You Need to Find
Determine whether you need to find force, acceleration, mass, or distance.

Step 3: Select the Appropriate Formula
Use F = G(Mm)/r² for gravitational force, or a = F/m for acceleration.

Step 4: Substitute and Calculate
Plug in values carefully. Use G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg² unless otherwise specified.

Example Problem: Calculate the gravitational force between two masses of 100 kg and 200 kg separated by a distance of 10 m.
Solution:
F = G × (M × m) / r²
F = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × (100 × 200) / (10)²
F = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 20,000 / 100
F = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 200
F = 1.334 × 10⁻⁸ N

Exam-Focused Tips for CBSE Class 9

To score well in gravitation questions during your term examinations and final board exams, keep these strategies in mind:

  • Practice at least 10-15 numerical problems to develop speed and accuracy.
  • Memorize the value of G (6.67 × 10⁻¹¹) and g (9.8 or 10 m/s²) without hesitation.
  • Always show unit conversions and intermediate steps in your answer sheet for full marks.
  • Draw diagrams for problems involving multiple objects or forces acting in different directions.
  • For theoretical questions, use examples from real life (satellites, planetary orbits) to enhance your answers.
  • Remember that gravitational force is always attractive and acts along the line joining the centers of mass.
