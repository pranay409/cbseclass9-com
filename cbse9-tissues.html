Tissues for CBSE Class 9: Plant and Animal Tissues Explained with Diagrams

Published on October 19, 2026 | NCERT Chapter 6

Tissues are one of the fundamental concepts in biology for CBSE Class 9. Understanding tissues is crucial not only for your Class 9 final exams but also for building a strong foundation for higher classes. In this comprehensive guide, we'll explore plant tissues, animal tissues, their types, functions, and key diagrams that frequently appear in CBSE exams.

What Are Tissues? – Definition and Basic Concept

A tissue is a group of similar cells that work together to perform a specific function. This concept is part of the hierarchical organization of life: cells → tissues → organs → organ systems → organisms.

Key Points for CBSE Exams:

  • Tissues are made up of cells of similar origin and structure
  • All cells in a tissue perform the same or related functions
  • Two main categories: Plant Tissues and Animal Tissues
  • This is covered in NCERT Class 9 Biology Chapter 6: Tissues
⚠️ Common CBSE Exam Mistake: Students often confuse between meristematic and permanent tissues. Remember: Meristematic tissues are actively dividing and found in growing regions, while permanent tissues have lost the ability to divide and perform specific functions. This distinction is frequently tested in CBSE Class 9 exams.

Plant Tissues: Types and Functions

Plant tissues are classified into two main categories based on their function and location:

1. Meristematic Tissues

Meristematic tissues are composed of young, actively dividing cells. They are responsible for plant growth and development.

Types of Meristematic Tissues:

  • Apical Meristem: Located at the tips of roots and shoots; responsible for vertical growth
  • Lateral Meristem: Found in the cambium; responsible for lateral/radial growth (increase in thickness)
  • Intercalary Meristem: Found between mature tissues; assists in growth of leaves and internodes

2. Permanent Tissues

Permanent tissues are composed of mature cells that have lost the ability to divide. They perform specialized functions. These are further classified as:

Tissue Type Location Function
Parenchyma Throughout the plant Storage, photosynthesis, wound healing
Collenchyma Below the epidermis of stems and leaves Support and flexibility in young growing parts
Sclerenchyma Around vascular bundles Mechanical support and rigidity
Xylem Vascular tissue Transport of water and minerals upward
Phloem Vascular tissue Transport of food (sugars) throughout plant

Animal Tissues: Types and Functions

Animal tissues are classified into four main types based on structure and function. This classification is essential for CBSE Class 9 Biology exams.

1. Epithelial Tissues

Epithelial tissues form the outer covering of the body and line the internal organs. They consist of tightly packed cells with minimal intercellular space.

Types:

  • Simple Cuboidal Epithelium: Cube-shaped cells; found in kidney tubules and ducts of glands
  • Simple Columnar Epithelium: Elongated cells; found in small intestine and stomach
  • Stratified Squamous Epithelium: Multiple layers; found in skin and mouth lining
  • Ciliated Epithelium: Cells with cilia; found in trachea and bronchi

2. Connective Tissues

Connective tissues bind and support other tissues and organs. They have cells dispersed in a matrix.

  • Bone: Hard, rigid connective tissue; provides structural support
  • Cartilage: Flexible; provides cushioning at joints
  • Blood: Fluid connective tissue; transports nutrients and oxygen
  • Adipose Tissue: Fat storage; provides insulation and energy

3. Muscular Tissues

Muscular tissues are responsible for movement in animals. They consist of elongated cells called muscle fibers.

  • Skeletal Muscle: Voluntary; attached to bones; produces movement
  • Cardiac Muscle: Involuntary; found in the heart; specialized for continuous contraction
  • Smooth Muscle: Involuntary; found in walls of internal organs

4. Nervous Tissues

Nervous tissues are responsible for receiving stimuli and transmitting impulses throughout the body. The basic unit is the neuron.

  • Neurons: Transmit nerve impulses
  • Glial Cells: Support and protect neurons
