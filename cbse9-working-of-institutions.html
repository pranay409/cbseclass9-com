Working of Institutions: Parliament, PM and Judiciary

Published: November 24, 2026 | Subject: Social Science (Civics) | Class: 9

Introduction: Understanding the working of democratic institutions is crucial for CBSE Class 9 Civics. This chapter, typically covered in NCERT Social Science Part II, focuses on how Parliament, the Prime Minister, and the Judiciary function together to maintain India's democratic framework. These three pillars form the backbone of our constitutional democracy and understanding their roles will help you score well in both periodic assessments and board exams.

1. Parliament: Structure and Functions (NCERT Chapter Reference)

Parliament is the supreme legislative body of India and is bicameral in structure. It consists of two houses: the Lok Sabha (House of the People) and the Rajya Sabha (Council of States). According to your NCERT Civics textbook, understanding Parliament's composition and powers is essential.

Lok Sabha

  • Members: Maximum 545 members, of which 530 represent states and 15 represent union territories
  • Duration: 5-year term; can be dissolved earlier
  • Eligibility: Citizen of India, minimum 25 years old, registered voter
  • Role: Directly elected by the people through universal adult suffrage; primary responsibility for money bills

Rajya Sabha

  • Members: Maximum 250 members; 238 from states and union territories, 12 nominated by the President
  • Duration: Permanent body; members serve 6-year terms with 1/3 retiring every 2 years
  • Eligibility: Minimum 30 years old, voter registration required
  • Role: Indirectly elected by state assemblies; represents states and ensures federal balance

The primary functions of Parliament include making laws, controlling finances through budget approval, and exercising oversight over the executive. For exam purposes, remember that all bills must pass both houses, though Lok Sabha has supremacy in financial matters.

2. The Prime Minister: Powers and Responsibilities

The Prime Minister is the head of government and the chief executive of India. While the President is the constitutional head of state, the PM exercises executive powers. This distinction is vital for exam success.

Key Powers of the PM

  • Council of Ministers: The PM selects and appoints ministers and can dismiss them at will
  • Legislative Authority: Proposes bills and directs government legislation in Parliament
  • Executive Control: Heads the cabinet and coordinates all government departments
  • Foreign Relations: Represents India in international matters and diplomacy
  • Emergency Powers: Can advise the President on declaring national emergencies

Accountability and Checks

The PM must retain the confidence of Lok Sabha. This is a critical point for your CBSE exam: the PM can be removed through a vote of no-confidence. This ensures that executive power remains accountable to the people's representatives. The PM must answer questions from Parliament regularly and present annual budgets for approval.

⚠️ Common Exam Mistake: Students often confuse the powers of the President and Prime Minister. Remember: the President is the constitutional head but the PM is the functional head. The President's powers are largely ceremonial, while the PM wields actual executive authority. This distinction appears frequently in CBSE exam questions.

3. The Judiciary: Structure and Independence

The Indian Judiciary is an independent arm of government responsible for interpreting laws and delivering justice. India follows a unitary judicial system with three tiers: the Supreme Court, High Courts, and District Courts.

Supreme Court

  • Composition: Chief Justice of India (CJI) and up to 33 other judges
  • Jurisdiction: Highest court of appeal; interprets the Constitution
  • Powers: Can issue writs (Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari) for protection of fundamental rights
  • Appointment: Judges appointed by the President on PM's recommendation; can serve until 65 years

High Courts and District Courts

  • High Courts: One in each state/union territory; supervise lower courts
  • District Courts: Trial courts handling civil and criminal cases
  • Hierarchy: Cases can be appealed to higher courts

Judicial Independence

A crucial feature emphasized in NCERT is judicial independence. The judiciary is protected from political pressure through several measures:

  • Judges cannot be removed easily; impeachment requires 2/3 majority in Parliament
  • Salaries fixed by law and cannot be reduced
  • Judges have security of tenure until retirement age
  • Courts can issue contempt orders against those who challenge judicial authority

The judiciary ensures that executive and legislative actions comply with the Constitution, making it the guardian of fundamental rights.

4. System of Checks and Balances (EXAM FOCUS)

The three institutions work in tandem through constitutional checks and balances. This concept is frequently tested in CBSE exams:

  • Parliament → Executive: Parliament can remove the PM through no-confidence motion; approves budgets and legislation
  • Executive → Parliament: PM proposes bills; President can return bills for reconsideration (though rare)
  • Judiciary → All: Courts can strike down unconstitutional laws or executive actions
  • Parliament → Judiciary: Can amend Constitution (with limits); impeach judges

This interdependence prevents any single institution from becoming too powerful, which is the essence of constitutional democracy.

Quick Revision Points for CBSE Exam

  • Lok Sabha = directly elected; Rajya Sabha = indirectly elected
  • PM is head of government (not President)
  • No-confidence motion can remove the PM
  • Supreme Court guards constitutional values
  • Checks and balances prevent concentration of power
  • Judicial review = power of courts to strike down unconstitutional laws

Conclusion: Understanding the working of institutions is fundamental to appreciating how Indian democracy functions. The interplay between Parliament, the PM, and the Judiciary ensures that power remains distributed and accountable to the people.
