Improvement in Food Resources: Crops and Animal Husbandry for Class 9

Published on October 27, 2026

Improvement in Food Resources is a crucial chapter in CBSE Class 9 Science that focuses on how we can increase food production to meet the growing needs of our population. This chapter covers both crop production (agriculture) and animal husbandry, exploring modern techniques and sustainable practices that help maximize yields while ensuring food security.

Understanding Food Resources and Population Growth

As our global population continues to grow exponentially, the demand for food has increased significantly. NCERT Class 9 Science emphasizes that improving food resources is not just about producing more crops or raising more animals—it's about doing so efficiently and sustainably. This chapter is fundamental to understanding how countries like India achieve food security while maintaining environmental balance.

The concept of food security involves ensuring that all people have access to sufficient, safe, and nutritious food. To achieve this, we need to improve both crop production and animal husbandry through scientific methods, better management practices, and technological advancements.

Crop Production and Improvement Techniques

According to NCERT Chapter 14 (Improvement in Food Resources), crop production improvement involves several key strategies:

1. Crop Variety Selection: Choosing the right variety of seeds is fundamental. High-yielding varieties (HYVs) of crops like wheat, rice, and maize have been developed through selective breeding and genetic improvement. These varieties produce more grain per plant and mature faster than traditional varieties.

2. Fertilizer Management: Proper use of organic and inorganic fertilizers provides essential nutrients like nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium (NPK) to crops. Organic fertilizers improve soil structure and microbial activity, while inorganic fertilizers provide quick nutrient availability. A balanced approach is recommended for sustainable farming.

3. Irrigation Systems: Adequate water supply through proper irrigation techniques ensures consistent crop growth. Methods like drip irrigation and sprinkler systems are more efficient than traditional flood irrigation, reducing water wastage.

4. Pest and Disease Management: Using integrated pest management (IPM) combines biological, chemical, and cultural methods to control pests and diseases. This reduces crop loss and ensures better yields.

Animal Husbandry and Livestock Management

Animal husbandry involves the care and breeding of livestock such as cattle, buffaloes, goats, sheep, and poultry. This section of the CBSE Class 9 curriculum focuses on how proper management can increase animal productivity:

Cattle and Buffalo Farming: These animals provide milk, meat, and leather. Improved breeds have higher milk-yielding capacity. Proper feeding, vaccination, and hygiene practices are essential for maintaining healthy herds and increasing productivity.

Poultry Farming: Chicken and eggs are important sources of protein. Modern poultry farming uses scientific methods to select high-laying varieties and provide optimal housing, nutrition, and disease management.

Fish Farming (Aquaculture): Fish and shellfish are excellent protein sources. Aquaculture involves breeding fish in controlled water environments, making it an efficient food production method that doesn't require large land areas.

📌 Key Concept: Common Mistakes to Avoid

Students often confuse the terms "subsistence farming" and "commercial farming." Subsistence farming produces food primarily for the farmer's own family consumption, while commercial farming aims to produce crops and livestock for sale in markets. In the context of food improvement, commercial agriculture with scientific methods plays a crucial role in meeting national food requirements. Understanding this distinction is important for exam questions.

Modern Agricultural Practices and Biotechnology

The NCERT textbook emphasizes that modern agriculture relies increasingly on biotechnology and scientific innovation. Genetically modified (GM) crops are developed to resist pests, tolerate herbicides, and provide better nutritional value. However, their adoption varies globally based on regulatory frameworks and public acceptance.

Precision farming, also known as site-specific crop management, uses technology like GPS, sensors, and data analytics to optimize input use and maximize yields. This approach reduces waste and environmental impact while improving profitability.

Organic farming practices, which avoid synthetic chemicals and rely on natural processes, are gaining popularity as consumers demand sustainable and chemical-free food products. Crop rotation, composting, and biological pest control are key elements of organic farming.

Exam-Specific Points to Remember

For your CBSE Class 9 Science examination, focus on these key areas from the Improvement in Food Resources chapter:

  • Definitions: Know what crop variety, high-yielding variety, integrated pest management, and animal husbandry mean.
  • Methods: Understand different irrigation methods, fertilizer types, and their applications.
  • Breeding: Grasp the concept of selective breeding and how it improves crop and animal productivity.
  • Case Studies: Be familiar with India's Green Revolution and how it transformed agricultural production.
  • Sustainability: Know about organic farming and sustainable practices that balance productivity with environmental conservation.
  • Problem-Solving: Be ready to answer questions about choosing appropriate methods for specific farming situations or environmental contexts.

This chapter typically appears as 3-5 mark questions in the CBSE Science board examination. Questions may ask you to define terms, explain processes, compare different methods, or solve practical farming scenarios. Practice drawing diagrams of different irrigation systems and labeling parts of farming setups, as visual representations often appear in exams.

Understanding "Improvement in Food Resources" not only helps you secure good marks but also develops awareness about agricultural practices, food security, and sustainable development—crucial knowledge for informed citizenship in an increasingly resource-conscious world.
