Number Systems for CBSE Class 9 Maths: Rational, Irrational and Real Numbers

Published on October 28, 2026

Number Systems is one of the fundamental chapters in CBSE Class 9 Mathematics (NCERT Chapter 1). This chapter forms the foundation for higher mathematics and is essential for understanding algebraic concepts, geometry, and trigonometry. In this guide, we'll explore rational numbers, irrational numbers, and real numbers with practical examples and exam-focused insights to help you master this critical topic.

Understanding Number Systems: NCERT Chapter 1 Overview

The Number System chapter in CBSE Class 9 introduces students to different classifications of numbers and their properties. According to the NCERT curriculum, students must understand the relationship between natural numbers, whole numbers, integers, rational numbers, irrational numbers, and real numbers.

Key concepts covered include:

  • Natural Numbers (N): 1, 2, 3, 4, ...
  • Whole Numbers (W): 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, ...
  • Integers (Z): ..., -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, ...
  • Rational Numbers (Q): Numbers that can be expressed as p/q where p and q are integers and q ≠ 0
  • Irrational Numbers: Numbers that cannot be expressed as p/q
  • Real Numbers (R): The union of rational and irrational numbers

This hierarchical classification is crucial for your CBSE exams, as questions often test your understanding of which category a given number belongs to. The NCERT textbook provides numerous worked examples that demonstrate these concepts with clarity.

Rational Numbers Explained with Exam Examples

A rational number is any number that can be expressed in the form p/q, where both p and q are integers and q is not equal to zero. This is the most common type of number you'll encounter in Class 9 mathematics.

Examples of rational numbers:

  • Positive fractions: 3/4, 5/8, 7/2
  • Negative fractions: -3/4, -5/8
  • Integers: 5 = 5/1, -3 = -3/1, 0 = 0/1
  • Terminating decimals: 0.5 = 1/2, 0.75 = 3/4, 0.125 = 1/8
  • Non-terminating repeating decimals: 0.333... = 1/3, 0.1666... = 1/6

In your CBSE exams, you'll often be asked to:

  • Identify whether a given number is rational or irrational
  • Find rational numbers between two given numbers
  • Represent rational numbers on the number line
  • Simplify and compare rational numbers

Important property: Between any two rational numbers, there exist infinitely many rational numbers. This means the rational number system is "dense," which is a key concept tested in CBSE exams.

⚠️ Common Exam Mistake

Students often confuse terminating decimals (0.5, 0.75) with non-terminating decimals. Remember: ANY terminating decimal can be expressed as p/q and is therefore rational. Non-terminating repeating decimals like 0.333... are also rational. Only non-terminating, non-repeating decimals are irrational!

Irrational Numbers: What Makes Them Special

An irrational number is a real number that cannot be expressed as p/q (where p and q are integers with q ≠ 0). The decimal representation of irrational numbers is non-terminating and non-repeating.

Common irrational numbers tested in CBSE Class 9:

  • Square roots of non-perfect squares: √2, √3, √5, √7 (approximately 1.414, 1.732, 2.236, 2.646)
  • Cube roots of non-perfect cubes: ∛2, ∛3, ∛5
  • The mathematical constant π (pi) ≈ 3.14159...
  • The mathematical constant e (Euler's number) ≈ 2.71828...
  • Special expressions like (√2 + √3), (1 + √5)/2 (golden ratio)

NCERT Chapter 1 provides proofs to show that √2 is irrational—this is a classic problem that appears frequently in CBSE examinations. The proof uses the method of contradiction and is essential to understand for your board exams.

Key operations with irrational numbers:

  • Rational + Irrational = Irrational
  • Irrational + Irrational = May be rational or irrational (e.g., √2 + (-√2) = 0)
  • Rational × Irrational = Irrational (except when rational is 0)
  • Irrational × Irrational = May be rational or irrational

Real Numbers and the Number Line: Integration and Applications

Real numbers (R) represent the union of rational and irrational numbers. In simpler terms: Real Numbers = Rational Numbers ∪ Irrational Numbers

This is a crucial relationship tested frequently in CBSE Class 9 exams. Every point on the number line corresponds to a real number, and every real number can be represented on the number line.

Key properties of real numbers:

  • Closure Property: The sum and product of two real numbers is always a real number
  • Commutative Property: a + b = b + a and a × b = b × a
  • Associative Property: (a + b) + c = a + (b + c) and (a × b) × c = a × (b × c)
  • Distributive Property: a(b + c) = ab + ac
  • Identity Elements: 0 for addition, 1 for multiplication
  • Inverse Elements: -a is additive inverse, 1/a is multiplicative inverse (for a ≠ 0)

The representation of real numbers on the number line is an important skill. Both rational and irrational numbers can be plotted using geometric construction methods. For example, √2 can be represented by constructing a right triangle with both legs equal to 1 unit.

In CBSE examinations, questions often ask you to locate irrational numbers between two integers on the number line or to identify which real numbers lie in a given range.
