Motion for CBSE Class 9: Distance, Displacement and Graphs Simplified

Published on October 21, 2026

Motion is one of the core topics in CBSE Class 9 Science (Chapter 8 in the NCERT textbook) that forms the foundation for understanding Physics. Whether it's a car moving down a highway, a ball rolling on the ground, or a satellite orbiting Earth, motion is everywhere. This comprehensive guide simplifies the key concepts of distance, displacement, and motion graphs—all essential for your board exams.

Understanding Distance vs. Displacement (NCERT Chapter 8)

Many students confuse distance and displacement, but understanding the difference is crucial for CBSE exams. Let's break it down:

Distance

Distance is the total path length covered by an object during motion. It's a scalar quantity, meaning it has only magnitude, no direction. Distance is always positive and never decreases as an object moves.

  • Example: If you walk 3 km north and then 4 km south, the total distance covered is 7 km.
  • Distance is measured in meters (m) or kilometers (km).
  • Distance depends on the path taken—different paths can give different distances.

Displacement

Displacement is the shortest straight-line distance between the initial and final positions of an object, along with its direction. It's a vector quantity, meaning it has both magnitude and direction. Displacement can be positive, negative, or zero.

  • Example: If you walk 3 km north and then 4 km south, your displacement is 1 km south (net change in position).
  • Displacement is independent of the path taken—only initial and final positions matter.
  • Displacement magnitude is always ≤ distance covered.

🎯 Key Tip for Exams

Common Mistake: Students often use "distance" and "displacement" interchangeably. Remember: a runner completing a 400m circular track has traveled 400m (distance) but has 0m displacement (returns to starting point). This type of question appears frequently in CBSE question papers!

Speed, Velocity, and Acceleration (NCERT Chapter 8)

Speed and velocity are related but distinct concepts that build on your understanding of distance and displacement.

Speed

Speed is the rate of change of distance. It's a scalar quantity and always positive.

Formula: Speed = Distance / Time

Unit: m/s or km/h

  • Average Speed: Total distance ÷ Total time
  • Instantaneous Speed: Speed at a particular instant

Velocity

Velocity is the rate of change of displacement. It's a vector quantity with both magnitude and direction.

Formula: Velocity = Displacement / Time

Unit: m/s (with direction)

  • Velocity can be positive or negative depending on the direction of motion.
  • For the same motion, velocity magnitude is always ≤ speed.

Acceleration

Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity. It's a vector quantity.

Formula: Acceleration = Change in Velocity / Time = (v - u) / t

Unit: m/s²

  • Positive acceleration: Velocity is increasing (speeding up)
  • Negative acceleration (Deceleration): Velocity is decreasing (slowing down)
  • Zero acceleration: Velocity is constant (uniform motion)

Motion Graphs Explained (NCERT Chapter 8)

Graphs are powerful tools to represent and analyze motion. The CBSE exam frequently includes questions on motion graphs. There are three main types:

Distance-Time (D-T) Graph

A distance-time graph shows how distance changes with time.

  • Straight horizontal line: Object is at rest (zero speed)
  • Straight diagonal line: Uniform motion (constant speed)
  • Curved line (getting steeper): Accelerated motion (speed increasing)
  • Curved line (getting less steep): Decelerated motion (speed decreasing)
  • Slope = Speed at any point

Velocity-Time (V-T) Graph

A velocity-time graph shows how velocity changes with time.

  • Straight horizontal line: Uniform velocity (zero acceleration)
  • Straight diagonal line: Uniform acceleration (constant acceleration)
  • Curved line: Non-uniform acceleration
  • Slope = Acceleration at any point
  • Area under the graph = Displacement
  • Line below x-axis: Motion in negative direction

Displacement-Time (S-T) Graph

Similar to distance-time graphs but shows displacement instead of distance. Displacement can be negative, so these graphs can extend below the x-axis.

📊 Exam Pattern Alert

In recent CBSE papers, questions often ask you to: (1) Interpret a given motion graph, (2) Calculate speed/acceleration from a graph's slope, (3) Find displacement using the area under a V-T graph, or (4) Draw your own graph for a described motion scenario. Practice all these variations!

Uniform and Non-Uniform Motion (NCERT Chapter 8)

Uniform Motion: An object moves with constant velocity (constant speed in a constant direction). The acceleration is zero. Distance covered in equal intervals of time is equal.

Non-Uniform Motion: An object's velocity changes over time. The acceleration is non-zero. Distance covered in equal intervals of time is unequal.

Most real-world motion is non-uniform. Your car accelerates and decelerates, a falling ball speeds up due to gravity—these are all examples of non-uniform motion, which is why understanding acceleration is so important.
