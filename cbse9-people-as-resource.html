People as Resource: CBSE Class 9 Economics

Chapter Notes, Concepts & Exam Questions | Published: November 27, 2026

People as Resource is Chapter 1 of the CBSE Class 9 Economics textbook under the Social Science curriculum. This chapter introduces students to the concept of human capital and how people become a valuable resource for economic development. Understanding this chapter is crucial for board exams and competitive entrance tests.

Understanding People as Resource: NCERT Chapter Overview

The NCERT economics textbook emphasizes that people are the most important resource of a nation. This concept goes beyond seeing individuals merely as consumers or workers—it recognizes that human beings possess skills, knowledge, and abilities that drive economic growth and development.

What Makes People a Resource?

People become a resource through investment in education, healthcare, and training. The term Human Capital refers to the stock of productive skills and technical knowledge embodied in people. Unlike physical capital (machines, buildings), human capital is intangible but immensely valuable.

Key Concept: Human Capital = Population + Education + Health + Skills + Experience

The NCERT chapter highlights that simply having a large population is not advantageous unless that population is educated, healthy, and skilled. India's demographic dividend can only be leveraged through proper investment in human development.

Human Capital Formation: NCERT Curriculum Focus

Human Capital Formation is the process by which people invest in themselves to improve their productivity and earning potential. The NCERT textbook identifies several key areas of investment:

1. Education and Training

Education is the most critical factor in human capital formation. It includes:

  • Formal education (schools, colleges, universities)
  • Informal education (learning from experience and peers)
  • Vocational training (skill-based programs)
  • On-the-job training

The CBSE Class 9 syllabus emphasizes that education increases earning potential and makes individuals more productive members of society. India's investment in education is crucial for achieving sustainable development goals.

2. Healthcare and Nutrition

Good health is essential for productivity. Healthcare and nutrition investments include:

  • Medical facilities and preventive healthcare
  • Nutritional programs for children and pregnant women
  • Vaccination and immunization drives
  • Awareness programs about hygiene and sanitation

A healthy population is more efficient and productive, leading to better economic outcomes. NCERT stresses that India's health indicators need significant improvement to fully utilize its human resources.

3. Experience and On-the-Job Training

Practical experience and skill development during employment enhance human capital. Workers learn new techniques, improve efficiency, and become more valuable to employers over time.

⚠️ Common Exam Mistake: Students often confuse "population" with "people as resource." A large population without education and health is a burden, not a resource. Always emphasize the quality of human capital in your CBSE Class 9 Economics answers.

Economic Contributions and Development: CBSE Exam Patterns

CBSE Class 9 examinations frequently ask students to explain how people contribute to economic development. Based on past board exam patterns, here are key points to remember:

Agricultural Productivity

Skilled farmers with modern knowledge and training can significantly increase agricultural output. Education about new farming techniques, crop rotation, and use of modern equipment is essential for rural development.

Industrial Growth

Trained workers, engineers, and managers drive industrial development. India's success in IT and service sectors demonstrates how investment in human capital generates employment and economic growth.

Service Sector Development

The service sector—including healthcare, education, tourism, and information technology—heavily relies on human capital. Well-trained professionals in these sectors attract foreign investment and create global opportunities.

Entrepreneurship and Innovation

Educated individuals are more likely to become entrepreneurs and innovators. They identify opportunities, take calculated risks, and create new businesses, contributing to job creation and economic growth.

India's Human Capital Challenges: Class 9 Economics Context

The NCERT chapter addresses challenges India faces in developing human capital:

  • High Dropout Rates: Many students, especially girls in rural areas, leave school prematurely, limiting their earning potential.
  • Malnutrition: Inadequate nutrition affects cognitive development and reduces productivity in later life.
  • Inadequate Healthcare: Limited access to medical facilities in rural India hampers health outcomes.
  • Skill-Knowledge Gap: Educational curriculum doesn't always align with industry requirements.
  • Gender Disparities: Girls' education and healthcare access remain challenging in many regions.

Understanding these challenges is essential for CBSE Class 9 examinations, as questions often ask students to identify problems and suggest solutions for human capital development.

Important Exam Questions & Answers

Q1: Define 'People as Resource' in the context of economics.

Answer: People as a resource refers to the population considered as human capital through investment in education, healthcare, and training. It emphasizes that people's productivity and contribution to economic development depend on their knowledge, skills, and health status—not merely on their numbers.

Q2: How does education contribute to human capital formation?

Answer: Education increases literacy rates, provides job-related skills, improves decision-making abilities, and enhances productivity. Educated individuals earn more, contribute more to economic growth, and can adapt to technological changes—all of which strengthen human capital.

Q3: What is the difference between population and people as resource?

Answer: Population refers to the total number of people in a country, while people as a resource means the population becomes valuable only when educated, healthy, and skilled. A large but uneducated population may be a liability; an educated and healthy population is an asset.

Q4: Name three factors that contribute to human capital formation.

Answer: (1) Education and training, (2) Healthcare and nutrition, (3) Experience and on-the-job training.
