Electoral Politics: Elections and Democracy Explained

Published on November 23, 2026 | CBSE Class 9 Study Guide

Electoral Politics is a fundamental concept in understanding how democracies function. For CBSE Class 9 Civics students, Chapter 2 of "Democratic Politics - I" explores the essential elements of elections, the electoral process, and why elections are crucial to maintaining democracy. This comprehensive guide will help you master Electoral Politics for your exams.

What is Electoral Politics and Why Does It Matter?

Electoral Politics refers to the system and processes through which citizens participate in selecting their representatives through voting. In a democracy like India, elections are the backbone of the political system. They determine who gets to make decisions on behalf of the people and ensure that power remains with the citizens.

According to the NCERT textbook for Class 9, elections serve several critical purposes:

  • Democratic participation: Elections allow every citizen to have a say in governance
  • Representation: Citizens elect representatives who will voice their concerns in legislative bodies
  • Accountability: Regular elections hold elected officials accountable to the people
  • Peaceful power transfer: Elections provide a constitutional way to change government without violence

In India's context, we follow a universal adult suffrage system where every citizen above 18 years of age has the right to vote, regardless of caste, religion, gender, or economic status. This principle was established by Dr. B.R. Ambedkar and other framers of our Constitution.

⚠️ Key Exam Tip: Always remember that universal adult suffrage in India applies to all citizens aged 18 and above. This was a revolutionary decision at the time of Independence, as many countries restricted voting rights based on education or property qualifications. This concept frequently appears in CBSE Class 9 board exams.

The Electoral System in India: Structure and Components

India follows a democratic electoral system with several key components that you must understand for your CBSE exams:

Delimitation of Constituencies

India is divided into electoral constituencies where each constituency elects one representative. This division ensures that all areas are represented equally. The delimitation process adjusts constituency boundaries periodically to maintain proportional representation based on population.

The Voter's Role

Voters are citizens registered in the electoral roll who have the right to cast their votes. The Election Commission of India maintains the electoral rolls which are regularly updated. Any Indian citizen above 18 years can register as a voter in their constituency.

Candidates and Political Parties

Political parties nominate candidates to contest elections. In India, candidates can also contest as independents (not representing any party). Candidates must meet specific eligibility criteria set by the Constitution and electoral laws.

The Election Commission

The Election Commission of India is a constitutional body responsible for conducting elections, ensuring fair play, and implementing electoral laws. It maintains independence from the government to prevent any biased interference in the electoral process.

Types of Elections and Electoral Processes (NCERT Chapter 2)

CBSE Class 9 Civics covers various types of elections conducted in India:

General Elections

These are conducted to elect members of the Lok Sabha (Parliament) and state assemblies. General elections determine which party will form the government at the national or state level. India conducts general elections every five years.

By-Elections

By-elections are held when a seat becomes vacant due to death, resignation, or disqualification of a sitting member. These elections are conducted only for that particular constituency.

Local Elections

Held for municipal corporations, municipal councils, and gram panchayats at the local level, these elections ensure democratic participation at the grassroots.

The typical electoral process in India involves:

  1. Announcement of election schedule by the Election Commission
  2. Nomination of candidates by political parties and independents
  3. Campaign period where candidates appeal to voters
  4. Polling day when voters cast their votes using Electronic Voting Machines (EVMs)
  5. Counting of votes and declaration of results

Challenges and Concerns in Electoral Politics

While elections are essential for democracy, there are several challenges in the Indian electoral system that CBSE exams frequently test:

  • Low voter turnout: In some regions, particularly urban areas, voter participation remains low due to apathy or inconvenience
  • Communal and caste-based voting: Some voters cast votes based on community or caste identity rather than merit or policies
  • Money power: Wealthy candidates and parties can spend more on campaigns, giving them unfair advantage
  • Criminalisation of politics: Some elected representatives have criminal backgrounds
  • Booth capturing: Illegal activities like intimidation or proxy voting undermine fair elections
  • Gender representation: Women are underrepresented in electoral politics despite reservation policies

To address these challenges, various reforms have been implemented including:

  • Use of EVMs to prevent ballot paper manipulation
  • Voter ID requirements to prevent proxy voting
  • Limits on campaign spending
  • Representation of Scheduled Castes (SCs) and Scheduled Tribes (STs) through reserved seats
  • 33% reservation for women in local bodies (Gram Panchayats and Municipal Corporations)
💡 Common Misconception: Students often confuse universal adult suffrage with universal literacy. Universal adult suffrage means all adults can vote regardless of education level, while universal literacy refers to educational attainment. For CBSE Class 9, remember that voting rights are not dependent on literacy or education qualifications.
