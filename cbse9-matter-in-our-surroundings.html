Matter in Our Surroundings for CBSE Class 9

Published on October 15, 2026

Matter is anything that occupies space and has mass. In CBSE Class 9 Science, Chapter 1 "Matter in Our Surroundings" from the NCERT textbook forms the foundation for understanding physical and chemical properties. This chapter is crucial for your board exams and lays the groundwork for higher chemistry studies. Let's explore the key concepts, states of matter, and common NCERT questions you'll encounter.

Understanding States of Matter (NCERT Chapter 1)

The NCERT textbook categorizes matter into three primary states: solid, liquid, and gas. Each state has distinct characteristics related to particle arrangement, intermolecular forces, and behavior under pressure and temperature.

Solids

Solids have:

  • Definite shape and definite volume
  • Particles closely packed with strong intermolecular forces
  • Particles vibrate in fixed positions
  • Incompressible nature

Examples: iron, wood, salt. According to NCERT, solids maintain their shape regardless of the container they're in, which is a key point for Class 9 exams.

Liquids

Liquids possess:

  • Definite volume but no definite shape
  • Particles close together with weaker intermolecular forces than solids
  • Particles can move freely
  • Nearly incompressible

Examples: water, milk, oil. The NCERT emphasizes that liquids take the shape of their container, making this distinction critical for understanding matter.

Gases

Gases have:

  • No definite shape or volume
  • Particles very far apart with minimal intermolecular forces
  • Particles move rapidly in all directions
  • Highly compressible

Examples: oxygen, nitrogen, carbon dioxide. Gas particles have the highest kinetic energy and travel at high speeds, a concept frequently tested in CBSE Class 9 exams.

💡 Key Tip for CBSE Exams

Remember the mnemonic for state properties: "Solids are Strict, Liquids are Liberal, Gases are Generous." Solids have fixed shape and volume, liquids have only fixed volume, and gases are free with both. This makes distinguishing between states quick and easy during exams.

Physical and Chemical Changes (NCERT Differentiation)

The NCERT Chapter 1 distinguishes between two types of changes in matter, and this distinction is essential for CBSE Class 9 assessments.

Physical Changes

Physical changes alter the appearance of matter but not its chemical composition:

  • Reversible nature: Can be reversed (e.g., melting ice → freezing water back)
  • No new substance: The chemical composition remains unchanged
  • Examples: boiling water, melting butter, crushing paper, cutting wood
  • Exam Focus: State changes (melting, boiling, condensation) are purely physical

Chemical Changes

Chemical changes transform matter into entirely new substances with different chemical properties:

  • Irreversible: Difficult or impossible to reverse
  • New substance formed: Chemical composition changes fundamentally
  • Examples: burning paper, rusting iron, digestion of food, fermentation
  • Exam Focus: Look for evidence like color change, gas release, or heat/light emission

CBSE Class 9 exams frequently ask students to classify changes as physical or chemical. The key is determining whether a new substance is formed. If molecules are rearranged but chemistry doesn't change, it's physical. If bonds break and new substances form, it's chemical.

Evaporation and Sublimation (Important NCERT Topics)

The NCERT textbook pays special attention to these processes, and they're common in CBSE question papers.

Evaporation

Evaporation is the process where liquid transforms into gas at temperatures below the boiling point. Key points from NCERT:

  • Occurs on the surface of liquids
  • Faster at higher temperatures
  • Rate increases with larger surface area and moving air
  • Endothermic process (requires heat energy)
  • Example: Wet clothes dry on the clothesline

Sublimation

Sublimation is the direct transition of a solid to gas without passing through the liquid state. NCERT examples include:

  • Dry ice (solid CO₂) turning into CO₂ gas
  • Iodine crystals converting to iodine vapor
  • Naphthalene (mothballs) evaporating

Both processes demonstrate that matter doesn't always follow the traditional solid → liquid → gas sequence, an important concept for CBSE Class 9 students.

⚠️ Common Mistake Students Make

Many students confuse evaporation with boiling. Remember: evaporation happens at the surface at any temperature below boiling point, while boiling occurs throughout the liquid at a specific temperature. During evaporation, only faster molecules escape, cooling the remaining liquid—this is why sweating cools your body, a classic NCERT example for Class 9.

Important NCERT Questions and Solutions for CBSE Class 9

Here are typical questions based on NCERT Chapter 1 that appear in CBSE Class 9 exams:

Question 1: Explain why solids are incompressible.

Answer: Solids are incompressible because their particles are already closely packed with strong intermolecular forces. There is no free space between particles, so external pressure cannot reduce their volume further.

Question 2: Differentiate between physical and chemical changes with two examples each.

Answer: Physical changes (reversible, no new substance): melting ice, boiling water. Chemical changes (irreversible, new substance): burning wood, rusting iron.

Question 3: Why does a gas exert pressure on the walls of a container?

Answer: Gas particles move randomly at high speeds in all directions. They continuously collide with container walls, creating pressure through these molecular collisions.

Question 4: What is the difference between evaporation and boiling?

Answer: Evaporation occurs at the surface below boiling point and is a slow process. Boiling occurs throughout the liquid at a fixed temperature and is a rapid process. Both are physical changes.

Question 5: Can a substance change directly from solid to gas? Give an example.

Answer: Yes, through sublimation. Dry ice (solid CO₂) directly converts to CO₂ gas without melting into liquid.

These NCERT-based questions represent the level of understanding expected from CBSE Class 9 students. Practice similar questions from your textbook to build confidence for board exams.
