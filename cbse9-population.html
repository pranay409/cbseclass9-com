Population: Density, Growth and Distribution

November 20, 2026

Population is a fundamental concept in geography that studies the characteristics, distribution, and dynamics of human populations. For CBSE Class 9 students, understanding population density, growth, and distribution is essential as it forms the basis for understanding how humans interact with their environment. This comprehensive guide covers all the key concepts from Chapter 6 of the NCERT Geography textbook "Contemporary India - I" to help you ace your exams.

Understanding Population Density (NCERT Chapter 6)

Population density refers to the number of people living per unit area of land. It is calculated using the formula: Population Density = Total Population ÷ Total Area. The density is typically expressed as the number of persons per square kilometer.

India's population density varies significantly across different regions. As of recent data, India's average population density is around 382 persons per km², making it one of the densely populated countries in the world. However, this density is not uniformly distributed. States like Bihar and West Bengal have very high densities (over 1000 persons/km²), while states like Arunachal Pradesh and Mizoram have significantly lower densities.

Factors affecting population density include:

  • Topography: Mountainous and hilly areas have lower population density compared to plains
  • Climate: Moderate climatic zones attract more population
  • Availability of resources: Areas with fertile soil, water, and mineral resources support higher populations
  • Development level: Industrialized and urban areas have higher population densities
  • Transportation and communication: Areas with better connectivity attract more people

⚡ Common Exam Tip

Don't confuse population with population density! Population refers to the total number of people, while density refers to the concentration per unit area. Many students mix these up in exams. Always remember: density = population ÷ area. Practice this formula with different state examples to master it.

Population Growth and Distribution (NCERT Chapter 6)

Population growth refers to the change in the size of a population over time. India's population has shown tremendous growth, particularly after independence. The growth rate depends on factors like birth rate, death rate, and migration.

Key Growth Indicators:

  • Birth Rate: Number of births per 1000 people per year
  • Death Rate: Number of deaths per 1000 people per year
  • Natural Growth Rate: Birth Rate - Death Rate
  • Migration: Movement of people from one place to another

India's population distribution is highly uneven. The Indo-Gangetic Plain, with its fertile soil and adequate water supply, is the most densely populated region. Conversely, the Thar Desert, the Himalayan Mountains, and the Northeast forests have relatively sparse populations. Major metropolitan areas like Delhi, Mumbai, Bangalore, and Chennai show extremely high concentrations of population due to economic opportunities and developed infrastructure.

Demographic Transition and Age-Sex Structure (NCERT Chapter 6)

Demographic transition theory explains how populations change over time. India is currently in the transition stage where both birth rates and death rates are declining, leading to slower population growth. The age-sex structure of a population reveals important information about its composition and can be represented through population pyramids.

India's population structure shows a larger proportion of younger people, which creates challenges in providing education, healthcare, and employment. Understanding this structure helps in planning for future resource allocation and development strategies.

Urbanization and Population Challenges (NCERT Chapter 6)

Rapid urbanization has led to significant population concentration in cities. This creates challenges including inadequate housing, insufficient infrastructure, environmental pollution, and traffic congestion. However, cities also offer opportunities for economic growth and development. India's urbanization rate is increasing, with more than 35% of the population now living in urban areas, and this percentage is expected to rise further.

Key challenges of rapid population growth:

  • Pressure on natural resources and environmental degradation
  • Inadequate housing, sanitation, and healthcare facilities
  • Rising unemployment and underemployment
  • Increased crime and social problems
  • Strain on agricultural productivity
  • Climate change impacts on vulnerable populations

To address these challenges, the Government of India has implemented various population control measures and development schemes focusing on education, women's empowerment, healthcare, and economic opportunities. These efforts have shown some success in controlling population growth rates over the decades.

Exam-Focused Summary and Practice Questions

Important definitions to memorize for exams:

  • Population Density: Number of persons per unit area
  • Population Growth: Change in population size over time
  • Birth Rate: Births per 1000 people annually
  • Death Rate: Deaths per 1000 people annually
  • Migration: Permanent movement of people
  • Urbanization: Process of increasing urban population

Expected exam questions:

  • What is population density? Calculate the density if population is 50 crores and area is 3.3 million km²
  • Explain the factors affecting population distribution in India
  • What are the challenges created by rapid population growth?
  • Compare population density of different Indian states
  • Discuss the relationship between urbanization and population growth
  • Explain what a population pyramid shows
