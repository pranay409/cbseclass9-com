Physical Features of India: Mountains, Plains and Plateaus

Published on November 16, 2026

India's physical geography is one of the most important topics in CBSE Class 9 Geography (Chapter 2: Physical Features of India). Understanding the mountains, plains, and plateaus that form India's landscape is essential not only for your board exams but also for competitive exams. This comprehensive guide covers all the key concepts you need to master.

Introduction to India's Physical Features

India is a vast country with diverse physical features spread across approximately 3.28 million square kilometers. The physical landscape of India can be divided into several major landforms: the Himalayan Mountains, the Indo-Gangetic Plains, the Peninsular Plateau, the Indian Desert, and the Coastal Plains.

Each of these major landforms has shaped India's climate, vegetation, soil types, and human settlement patterns. For CBSE Class 9, you must understand the characteristics, location, and significance of each landform.

⚠️ Common Mistake Alert

Many students confuse the difference between Mountains and Plateaus. Remember: Mountains are elevated regions with steep slopes and peaks, while plateaus are elevated flat-topped areas. The Deccan Plateau is NOT a mountain range, though it lies at high elevation.

The Himalayan Mountain System

The Himalayas form the northern boundary of India and are the youngest and highest mountain range in the world. This massive system extends over 2,400 km from west to east and covers an area of about 500,000 square km.

Key Characteristics of the Himalayas:

  • Age: Geologically young mountains (formed during the Tertiary period)
  • Height: Average height of 6,000 meters with peaks exceeding 8,000 meters
  • Snow-capped peaks: Permanent snow on higher altitudes
  • Steep slopes: Subject to heavy erosion and landslides

Three Main Divisions:

The Himalayas are divided into three parallel ranges from north to south:

  1. Greater Himalayas (Himadri): The northernmost and highest range, containing peaks like Mount Everest (Sagarmatha), K2, and Kangchenjunga.
  2. Lesser Himalayas (Himachal): Located between Greater Himalayas and the Plains, with heights between 1,000-4,000 meters. This range is known for hill stations like Shimla and Mussoorie.
  3. Outer Himalayas (Shiwaliks): The southernmost range, made of loose sedimentary rocks and prone to erosion. Heights range from 600-1,500 meters.

Significance of the Himalayas:

  • Act as a barrier protecting India from cold Arctic winds
  • Influence India's climate and monsoon patterns
  • Source of major rivers like the Ganges, Brahmaputra, and Indus
  • Rich biodiversity and unique flora-fauna
  • Important for India's water resources

The Indo-Gangetic Plains

The Indo-Gangetic Plains extend from the mouth of the Indus River in the west to the mouth of the Ganges in the east, covering an area of about 42,000 square km. These are the most densely populated plains in the world.

Characteristics of the Plains:

  • Formation: Formed by the deposition of alluvial soil by major rivers
  • Topography: Extremely flat with very gentle slopes
  • Soil: Highly fertile alluvial soil, ideal for agriculture
  • Elevation: Generally less than 300 meters above sea level
  • Width: Varies from 150 km to 300 km

Divisions and Key Rivers:

The plains can be divided into three sections:

  • Indus Plains: Located in Pakistan (western part), drained by the Indus River
  • Gangetic Plains: The largest section, drained by the Ganges River and its tributaries. This region is the agricultural heartland of India and home to major cities like Delhi, Lucknow, and Kolkata.
  • Brahmaputra Plains: Located in Assam, drained by the Brahmaputra River. Known for tea gardens and high rainfall.

Significance for India:

  • India's agricultural breadbasket - produces majority of food grains
  • Supports India's largest population concentration
  • Major centers of commerce, industry, and culture
  • Abundant water resources from rivers

The Peninsular Plateau

The Peninsular Plateau is a large, elevated, relatively flat landmass covering an area of about 16 lakh square km, occupying about half of India's total area. It is the oldest landform in India, with origins dating back to the Precambrian era.

Key Features:

  • Age: Geologically ancient (over 2 billion years old)
  • Height: Ranges from 300 to 900 meters above sea level
  • Shape: Triangular with a broad north and narrow south (Cape Comorin)
  • Terrain: Tableland with flat-topped hills and gorges
  • Plateau edges: Can have steep slopes (called escarpments)

Major Plateau Regions:

  • Deccan Plateau: The largest plateau, covering much of southern India. It is a lava plateau formed by volcanic activity.
  • Western Ghats: Mountain range running parallel to the western coast with an average height of 1,000-1,500 meters. It acts as a barrier to the southwest monsoon.
  • Eastern Ghats: Lower and more discontinuous than the Western Ghats, with heights between 600-900 meters.

Characteristics of the Peninsular Plateau:

  • Contains mineral-rich rocks (iron ore, manganese, bauxite, limestone)
  • Major source of India's mineral wealth
  • Lower rainfall compared to surrounding areas
  • Dense forests in some regions (Western Ghats)
  • Major rivers like Krishna, Godavari, and Narmada originate here

📌 Important for Exams

Remember the three main divisions of India's physical features: Northern Mountains (Himalayas), Central Plains (Indo-Gangetic), and Southern Plateau (Peninsular Plateau). CBSE frequently asks MCQs about which region produces what (e.g., "Which plateau is rich in mineral resources?"). The answer is always the Peninsular/Deccan Plateau.
